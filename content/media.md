### Full bio

Luke Drake is an assistant professor of Classical Studies at BYU in Provo and of Near Eastern Studies at the BYU Jerusalem Center. He received a PhD in ancient Mediterranean religions from the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill, a Master of Theological Studies in the New Testament and Early Christianity from Harvard Divinity School, and a Bachelor of Arts at BYU in English literature. His research centers on early Christian literature and history, and his work has been published in academic journals such as the *Journal of Early Christian Studies* (Johns Hopkins Press), *Vigiliae Christianae* (Brill), and *New Testament Studies* (Cambridge University Press). He is a volume editor of Bloomsbury's upcoming *Cultural History of Religion in Antiquity* and he acted as general editor of *The Emergence of Christianity*, the third and final volume of collected essays of the Swiss biblical scholar, François Bovon.

### Conversation starters

Questions hosts often find fruitful, drawn from the book:

- What was it like to live in a city where the emperor was worshipped as a god?
- Why are there four Gospels, and what makes each one distinct?
- How does knowing that Paul's letters were real letters, to real congregations, change how we read them?
- What does ancient Corinth tell us about the first Christian communities?
- How should a Latter-day Saint approach apocalyptic books like Revelation?
